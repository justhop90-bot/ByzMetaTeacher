# Fortification, Siege, Monk, and Late-Game Behavioral Contract

## Fortification

Defensive infrastructure is justified by exposure, geometry, threat, age, resource availability, and opportunity cost.

The current strategy may prove that defensive capability is required and reuse construction lifecycle.

Exact placement remains empirical and engine-dependent.

### Required categories

- walls/gates;
- Outposts;
- tower upgrades;
- Castles;
- late Bombard Towers.

Placement-sensitive assumptions must not be elevated into compiler facts.

## Siege

Siege is a conversion capability, triggered by:

- sustained ranged mass;
- fortified structures;
- production denial;
- Castle/TC pressure;
- late positional conversion.

The stock strategy exposes:

- Siege Workshop capability;
- Mangonel support;
- Bombard Cannon conversion.

Workshop existence is not siege completion.

Siege-unit existence is not battlefield success.

## Monks

The strategy owns:

- Monastery capability;
- bounded Monk support;
- Monk replacement.

Relic acquisition remains OPEN until native discovery, targeting, transport, and completion semantics are independently proven.

## Imperial conversion

Imperial Age triggers a new strategic arbitration pass.

The controller reassesses:

- food throughput;
- production capacity;
- upgrade packages;
- siege;
- fortification;
- Monks;
- water continuation/shutdown;
- economic expansion.

Late game is not complete because the age-up completed.

## Strategic release

A late-game package is released when:

- its technology completes;
- its role is no longer useful;
- its target ceases to exist;
- the higher-level posture changes;
- the economy can no longer justify it and emergency policy deliberately displaces it.

## Acceptance

Late-game behavior requires:

- explicit strategic reason;
- typed capability;
- actual provider;
- world-state witness;
- opportunity-cost protection;
- recovery;
- deterministic lowering;
- native acceptance.
